About Project

Raksh is a redesigned space diaper for urine management during ISRO's Gaganyaan mission, built through iterative ergonomic and material research to offer astronauts a mess-free, comfortable way to handle hygiene in microgravity.

Limitations of Current MAG Systems
01

MAGs only absorb waste — they do not actively remove or separate it, so astronauts remain in contact with urine until the suit is doffed.

02

Extended contact with absorbed waste can cause skin irritation, rashes, and increase risk of urinary tract infections or gastrointestinal distress.

03

Current MAGs do not fully contain odors, which can affect appetite and overall crew comfort in enclosed suit environments.

04

As disposable adult diapers, MAGs generate additional solid waste that must be stored and returned to Earth, complicating logistics on long missions.

05

Early attempts to design female-specific containment devices largely failed, and even current MAGs can be uncomfortable or ill-fitting across different body types.

User Research
Experiment 1

User Experience Study: Pull-Up Adult Diaper in Astronaut-Like Lying Position

Test Setup

Body Position: Lying on the back with knees bent and feet elevated — mimicking the seated recline posture of astronauts inside a spacecraft.

Purpose

Astronauts often rely on diapers during launch, long missions, or spacewalks where bathroom access is impossible. This experiment aimed to simulate the astronaut body position during launch and evaluate the diaper's usability, comfort, and performance.

Wearing the Pull-Up Diaper and Urinating in the Diaper

Testing the pull-up diaper in a lying, astronaut-like posture revealed it was hard to wear without lifting the hips, and liquid distributed unevenly once in use — pointing to a need for stretchable panels and better fluid dispersion, while confirming the wetness indicator was a valuable feature to keep.

Removing and Disposing the Diaper
Experiment 2

Testing an Adult Diaper: Capacity, Comfort & Leakage

Material testing (SAP-based diaper vs. liquid capacity, leakage, and post-use comfort under a 1L high-flow pour) confirmed strong absorption but revealed leakage under pressure and long dry-time — pointing to the need for better fluid distribution in the final design.

Experiment 3

Testing Absorption & Comfort: Diaper vs Sanitary Pads Using Lemon Water (Acidic Test)

Comparing an adult diaper against sanitary pads under acidic-liquid testing showed the diaper had the highest capacity and reliability, while both pads struggled to hold pressure or distribute liquid effectively.

Concept — RAKSH

Final Concept, Out of All the Concepts

Raksh is a two-part system: a reusable outer Trunk with a hydrophobic layer for double-leak protection, and a disposable inner Diaper with a hydrophobic top layer that channels moisture away from skin, a SAP core for absorption, a leak-proof base, and an integrated disposal pocket for easy, mess-free changes.

Inspiration

Baby Carriers Meet Adult Diapers

Raksh draws on baby carriers for snug, ergonomic body support and adult diaper systems for leak-proof, body-conforming absorption — merging both into a compact, passive wearable solution for waste containment in microgravity.

Inspiration of Trunk from: Transfer Pants Adaptive Shorts.
Inspiration of Waist Belt from: Expert Belts.

Research

The diaper uses a four-layer material system — soft nonwoven top, SAP/SAF absorbent core with odor control, and a TPU leak-proof base — while the trunk pairs a microfiber inner layer with a hydrophobic, water-resistant outer shell for full containment.

Inner layer — Microfiber Terry or PUL (Polyurethane-Laminated Fabric).
Outer layer — Hydrophobic Nylon or Polyester with DWR Coating.
Trunks — Hydrophilically treated PET with 3D texture for added dryness (optional material).

Prototype
Future Scope

Future work extends Raksh to fecal waste management, gender-specific fit, and sustainable materials — with broader potential in eldercare, mobility support, and long-duration missions like lunar or Mars exploration.

Conclusion

By narrowing the focus to urine management, Raksh reimagines a small but critical part of astronaut life in microgravity — grounded entirely in the astronaut's real experience and needs.
