Why young adults keep scrolling even when they know it's draining them — and a calmer way to stop.

Research Overview

This research explores why young adults keep compulsively scrolling Instagram Reels even when they know it's harmful. Using interviews, surveys, and other methods, it uncovers a "restlessness-relief-fatigue" loop fueled by low-value "brain rot" content, escapism, and social obligation — revealing the gap between wanting to stop and actually being able to.

Read the Analytical Report ↗
Problem Statement

Aware it's draining them, unable to stop

Young adults aged 18–24 are aware that their doomscrolling habits are compulsive and mentally draining, yet they struggle to stop because the high-dopamine, low-value content on social media keeps them engaged. They need a constructive substitute that offers the same sense of escape and stimulation, but through purposeful, value-driven content rather than empty, mindless scrolling.

Designing for Intentional Pauses

Instagram introduces a "Calm Mode" that activates when restless, repetitive scrolling is detected — nudging users with "Need a breather? Switch to Calm Mode." This shifts them into a self-curated, distraction-free feed built only from content they've chosen in advance (like photography, design inspiration, or close friends). The feed isn't infinite — it naturally closes after 15–20 posts, offering a pause that feels deliberate and restorative instead of just another scroll hole.

Primary Research

a) Physical Interviews

15 semi-structured interviews

In-person interviews exploring Instagram usage habits, emotional responses, and digital fatigue.

b) Online Surveys

35 anonymous responses

Google Forms responses on usage frequency, self-reported fatigue, emotional impact, and regulatory behaviours.

c) Instagram Story Polls

400 responses

A Stories poll ("What do you use Instagram for?") tracking personal, content-creation, business, and shopping use via Instagram Insights.

Secondary Research

Research focused on habit-loop psychology, addictive design patterns, digital fatigue, and emotional dependency on social media.

Key Insights
01
Hours Spent on Instagram
Instagram use is highly fragmented across the day, but disproportionately concentrated at night due to lowered self-regulation and lack of accountability.
02
Routine and Habit Formation
Instagram has evolved from a voluntary check-in to a reflexive bedtime ritual, triggered without conscious intention.
03
Content Type
Low-information "brain-rot" content produces high dopamine but yields guilt and mental fatigue, whereas high-utility content does not.
04
Purpose of Using Instagram
Instagram has shifted from personal use to a social obligation — a space users feel they must occupy.
05
Self-Realisation
Users possess strong self-awareness of overuse but cannot convert that awareness into behaviour change.
06
Content Value & Perception
Users justify continued usage by pointing to pockets of "valuable content" (tech, design, reflective posts), even though most consumed content is low-value.

From Restless Scroll to Focus Feed

Concept

Our Concept

When restlessness kicks in, the app asks: "Feeling restless? Try Focus Mode." Saying yes drops you into a minimal, ad-free, distraction-free feed of only what you've pre-selected — photography, design, close friends. It's capped at 15–20 posts, so it ends on its own, leaving you refreshed and in control instead of guilty and drained.

Rationale

Why This Solution

It works because it strips away distractions and endless choice — a personalized, ad-free feed with a built-in stopping point. That structure turns scrolling into an intentional break rather than a guilt-inducing spiral, supporting mental clarity, lower anxiety, and genuine control over social media use.

Outcome

Expected Impact

Sharper focus and less digital fatigue, thanks to a short, personalized feed that replaces endless scrolling with intentional breaks. The result is healthier habits, better mental clarity, and more mindful, guilt-free engagement.
