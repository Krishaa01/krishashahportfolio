RANI NO HAJIRO

A field study of memory, heritage, and everyday life around Ahmedabad's Rani no Hajiro market.

What is Rani no Hajiro?

A resting place of queens

Rani no Hajiro is a historical place in the old city of Ahmedabad. It is the resting place of the queens from the time of the Gujarat Sultanate, built around the year 1445. The tombs are made of marble and have beautiful carvings, showing a mix of Hindu, Jain, and Islamic designs. Some families still live near the tombs and take care of them. It is a peaceful space with a strong connection to the past.

Market Around Rani no Hajiro

A market that keeps the heritage alive

Right outside the tomb complex, there is a busy and colorful market. It is famous for selling women's clothes, jewelry, and Garba dresses during festivals. Many small stalls are set up, and you can also find mouth fresheners called Mukhwas. People from all over the city come here to shop, especially during festivals like Navratri. This market brings life to the area and helps keep the heritage of the place alive through everyday activity.

Research Question

What we set out to understand

How does Rani no Hajiro market contribute to the cultural and historical identity of Old City (Ahmedabad), and how do social and economic factors impact its traditional essence and long-term viability?

Field Notes

Insights and Observations

Observation

They were concerned that we weren't students and that some of us were reporters who would alert the authorities — because the location where they set up their stalls is illegal and the government has proposed two FIRs.

Insight

The family is anxious and distrustful of outsiders due to ongoing eviction threats. They rely on customers, but fear that media or official attention could lead to displacement — showing how visibility in informal economies is both vital and risky.

Observation

Despite being a seasonal market, they operate their business full time since they depend on their devoted clients due to their generational ownership.

Insight

The market's generational ownership makes vendors rely on loyal customers, keeping it active year round. Stability matters more than expansion, as their inherited trade is their main livelihood. This shows how tradition and necessity shape their business choices.

Observation

The government is not helping in looking after the heritage as the caretakers have taken sole responsibility on themselves, and they do not take a stand for themselves as their house is demolished.

Insight

With their home demolished and family members sick, the caretakers have no choice but to stay. Leaving for other work means losing the only shelter they have — the tomb they maintain. This highlights how heritage preservation for them is not a job but a means of survival.

Outcome

A zine, translated from the field

The final output of this project is a zine that translates our research on Rani no Hajiro into an accessible, visual narrative — bringing together the personas, spatial mapping, and everyday rhythms of the place into a single tactile object.

Why Zine?

Research you can hold

A zine let us present research in a way that's accessible and engaging, rather than locking it into a formal academic report.
