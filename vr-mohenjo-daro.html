Project Overview

Uncovering a lost city, one brick at a time

A multisensory VR experience that takes the user through the process of uncovering Mohenjo-daro. The experience starts with the user working as a labourer, breaking bricks and collecting artifacts, before shifting into the role of an archaeologist examining the discoveries. The experience uses VR and physical interaction to explore different theories about what happened to the ancient city.

The Concept

Why this story

Colonial railway builders stripped bricks from Mohenjo-daro's ancient ruins to lay the 1870 Lahore-Karachi line — a stark example of how careless development destroys irreplaceable archaeology.

This "brick theft" story bridges ancient civilization and colonial history, making a powerful, emotional lesson in why heritage preservation matters.

British Colonial Era · 1856 CE

British engineers of the East India Company discover large baked bricks while building the East Indian Railway. Mohenjo-daro was rediscovered when railway workers collected ancient bricks. Artifacts were ignored, and the site was unintentionally damaged.

Archaeological Survey Era · 1920–1922

Official excavations begin at Mohenjo-daro under the Archaeological Survey of India. The site is now protected, researched, and preserved — highlighting how our approach to heritage has changed.

Walkthrough

User Experience Flow

Scene 01
The Worksite

You open your eyes on a brick-strewn dig site. A newspaper from the era lies at your feet, and the Saheb's orders echo around you. There's nothing else.

Scene 02
First Discoveries

As you lift bricks, you uncover pottery shards, tools, and bone fragments. Every find brings a louder scolding from the Saheb, but throwing them away feels wrong.

Scene 03
The Table

You quietly gather every find onto a workbench. The Saheb's voice fades, and for the first time the pieces sit together as a city in fragments. You pick up a brush and dust one object. Mid-stroke, your hands change, the railway noise falls silent, and the world rebuilds around them.

Scene 04
Modern Mohenjo-daro

The same site now sits fenced and silent, with markers where the bricks were taken. The Saheb is gone, and that absence says everything.
